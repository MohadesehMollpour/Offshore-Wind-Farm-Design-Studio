# Assessment traceability

The supplied `Assessment Brief.pdf` is the formal 2026/27 assignment. PDF page 1 requests a fictitious offshore wind farm business proposal supported by technical information across the whole lifecycle. Page 2 requires an integrated group report, section leads, documented assumptions and data sources, and AI tools disclosed by section in the contribution table.

Deadline: **Thursday 17 December 2026, 14:00 Europe/London**. The software is a working aid and optional extension. It is not the submitted assessment report.

Official rubric, worksheet **Assessment Criteria**, labels A4–A7 and descriptors B4:K7:

| Criterion | Weight | Implementation implication |
|---|---:|---|
| Quality of Analysis | 40% | Appropriate analysis, justified assumptions, uncertainty and supported conclusions |
| Coherence | 20% | Trace dependencies, reconcile energy/cost assumptions and explain trade-offs |
| Identification and use of data sources | 20% | Multiple suitable sources, source limitations, consistency checks and interests |
| Organisation and presentation | 20% | Integrated report, readable visuals, consistent units, language and references |

The top-band descriptors were read from the workbook. The original workbook remains unmodified. No automated mark has been assigned.

The 2023 Viking sample is a historical example. Its claims and assumptions are not 2026 site evidence. The current AI rule should not be retroactively used to grade that report. CCS, offshore oil/gas reuse, detailed grid congestion and the web platform are proposed extensions from the master prompt, rather than additional formal rubric categories.
